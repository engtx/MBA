STP is a guided way to set a company’s aspirations: what value you stand for, which customers that value is for, and how you show up against competitors. It goes Segment -> Target -> Position, and you have to do it in that order. Segmenting is looking at the whole market and grouping people by a common set of needs. Targeting is deciding which of those groups you want to serve, or are actually able to serve. Positioning is writing the value proposition so those customers know why you’re the better choice. A critical point is tht you are not creating segments. You’re uncovering them with market data and customer intelligence.

I’ll use the last thing I bought: Pampers AMORE diapers. Pampers isn’t treating the diaper aisle as one mass market. They look at demographics (income, new parents), psychographics (health-conscious, “I only want the best for my baby”), and benefits sought (sensitive skin, dryness, no fragrance, no tradeoff between gentle and actually works). Those needs were already there. AMORE is built for the parents who want premium materials and hospital-level trust, and who will pay for it.

That target includes families like mine. We’re attractive because if quality stays high, we keep buying for a couple of years — a pretty stable income stream — and there are a lot of other high-earning, health-conscious parents in the same boat. The high price is on purpose. It reserves this line for people like us and quietly excludes parents shopping mainly on cost (Kirkland, generic, or even regular Swaddlers). P&G still serves those other groups with different lines. AMORE is the “no compromise” tier.

Relative to competitors, AMORE is positioned as Pampers best diaper, against Huggies, and Coterie. Against Pampers own Pure line it’s the upgrade. The story is basically: Parent who are health concious and won't compromise on quality choose AMORE.
