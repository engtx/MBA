# Week 2 — STP, the 5 Cs, and the 4 Ps

STP is not part of the marketing mix. It is the decision that **comes first** and tells the mix what to do.

Dolan’s process is a stack:

**5 Cs (analysis) → STP (aspiration) → 4 Ps (action plan) → outcomes**

The 5 Cs tell you what is true. STP decides **what value, for whom**. The 4 Ps are how you **create and capture** that value. Kotler’s line from the Week 1 reading: once positioning is solved, the mix is “the tactical details of the positioning strategy.”

Related notes: [Week 2 Slides Takeaways](Week%202%20Slides%20Takeaways.md).

---

## What STP actually is

**STP = Segment → Target → Position.** It is the **aspiration decision**: *What value will we represent, to which customers, against which alternatives?*

| Step | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Segment** | How does the market naturally group? | Homogeneous subgroups with similar needs (discovered, not invented) |
| **Target** | Which of those groups will we serve? | One or more segments you can reach, serve, and make money in |
| **Position** | What should they think of us vs. the alternatives? | A value proposition + a sentence the whole firm can use |

Skip a step and the next one gets sloppy. If you do not segment, targeting is “everyone.” If you do not target, positioning is a slogan with no audience. If you do not position, the 4 Ps have nothing to align to, and customers will position you themselves.

---

## Where it sits (and what it is not)

People often mash “marketing mix” and “strategy” together. In this course they are different layers:

```
ANALYSIS          5 Cs: customer, company, collaborators, competition, context
                      ↓
ASPIRATION        STP: what value to whom
                      ↓
ACTION PLAN       4 Ps: product, promotion, place (create value)
                       price (capture value)
                      ↓
OUTCOMES          acquire → retain → brand ambassadors
                  sales, profit, franchise
```

- The **5 Cs** are research and judgment. They do not pick a strategy by themselves.
- **STP** is the choice. It excludes people on purpose.
- The **4 Ps** are execution of that choice. They are the mix. STP is not a fifth P.

Week 2 class put it as: *What value do we offer, and to whom?* That is STP. The mix answers *how*.

## How the 5 Cs feed STP

You cannot segment or target from a blank page. Each C constrains a different part of STP.

**Customer (center).** This is the raw material for **segmentation**. Who is in the DMU? What is the DMP? What needs, usage, and “why they buy” exist? Gupta’s three cuts — *who they are / what they have done / why they buy* — all come from customer analysis. If you skip this, you default to easy demographics and get Bic-for-Her mistakes.

**Company.** This gates **targeting**. A segment can be large and growing and still be wrong if you cannot serve it. Core competency (hard to copy + actually creates perceived value) is the test. Peloton was not “a bike company”; it targeted time-pressed riders because it could deliver studio intensity at home. Broadcom does not target phone buyers; it targets Apple’s need for lighter/smaller/faster silicon, because that is the skill set.

**Collaborators.** Affects **whether a target is accessible** and how you will later set **place**. Can a partner reach the segment you cannot? Peloton used manufacturers so it could focus on content and education. If you pick a segment you can only reach through a retailer that owns the relationship, that collaborator will claim a lot of the value.

**Competition.** Shapes **targeting and positioning**. Value is always vs. the next-best alternative (Gupta’s EVC). Class’s four levels matter here:

- brand (other carbon-fiber custom bikes in the sim)
- category (traditional high-end bikes)
- generic (any way to ride / get fit)
- total budget (other $3k–$5k toys)

You target where rivals are weak or where you can be the distinctive answer. You position against a **frame of reference** you choose (Mountain Dew vs. all CSDs, not vs. coffee).

**Context.** Tells you which segments are **stable** and which positions will hold. Tech, culture, regulation, and the natural environment change what “value” means. Orbitz’s Mac vs. PC hotel sort was a context + data play; it also shows the ethics line Gupta flags.

**How they lock together when you pick a target** (Week 2 slide):

| Segment | Company | Competition |
|---|---|---|
| Size, growth, spend, profit after cost-to-serve | Can we reach them? Do we want to? What does it cost us to deliver the value? | How intense? How happy are they now? Barriers? |

All three have to fit. Attractive + we can serve it + room to win.

## How STP feeds the 4 Ps

Positioning is a sentence. The mix is that sentence made real.

Class template:

> Among **[target]**, **[brand]** is the **[frame of reference]** that **[point of difference]** because **[reason to believe]**.

Dolan’s version is the same idea: target, their want, category as they see it, key benefit.

That sentence assigns jobs to each P:

| P | Role vs. STP | If STP is sloppy |
|---|---|---|
| **Product** | The bundle that delivers the point of difference (Levitt’s total product: core + services + brand + support). Features → advantages → **benefits**. | You add features (feature fatigue) instead of solving the target’s problem. |
| **Promotion** | Says the position to the right people, in the right media (6 Ms: Market and Mission come from STP). | You buy mass media for a niche, or talk features to people who buy on status. |
| **Place** | Puts the offer where that segment actually buys and gets service. Accessibility of the segment. | You pick a channel that cannot reach them, or that dilutes the position (luxury in a discounter). |
| **Price** | Captures part of the value you created. Ceiling = perceived value vs. next-best alternative. | You cost-plus price a premium target, or premium-price a value segment. |

Shapiro’s bar for the mix: **consistency** (they fit), **integration** (they help each other), **leverage** (each P used where it is strongest). That is only possible if STP is one story.

**Create vs. capture:** Product + promotion + place create the value the target cares about. Price takes some of it. If STP is “premium customization for cachet-seeking riders,” you do not then race to the lowest price. If STP is “smart value for the price-sensitive tea buyer,” you do not wrap it in a luxury tin.

## Worked through one example

**Peloton (Dolan):**

- **5 Cs:** Customer = affluent, time-pressed, wanted SoulCycle energy without the studio. Company = tech + content, not “make bikes.” Collaborators = bike/tablet manufacturers, capital. Competition = SoulCycle, basement treadmills, *and* not working out. Context = social media, at-home fitness.
- **S:** Time-pressed home riders vs. studio tribalists vs. cheap-equipment buyers.
- **T:** Start with affluent suburban; later reframe price (financing) when they found a broader group who still valued the product.
- **P:** “World-class indoor cycling, on your time, at home.”
- **4 Ps:** Product = bike + instructors + music + community (the bundle). Promotion = showrooms + social, not “we sell bikes.” Place = owned showrooms + DTC (education was too hard for a retailer). Price = high sticker, then $58/month framing so it still fit the target’s economics.

The mix only makes sense **after** that STP. A $2,000 bike in Dick’s, advertised on price, would have been a different (weaker) strategy.

**Broadcom is the B2B version:** you do not segment “phone users” as *your* customers. You segment Apple’s needs (and Apple’s consumers one step downstream). Target = design wins where lighter/smaller/faster matters. Position = the part that lets Apple keep *its* customers. Then product (roadmap), place (design-in), promotion (to Apple’s DMU), price (EVC vs. the next chip vendor).

**Sim Q1** is the same stack with almost no mix yet: use the survey (customer C), pick **one** segment (S+T), open **one** outlet (a tiny place decision). Brand, ads, and price wait until Q2 because you should not design those until the target is chosen.

## A sequence you can reuse (quiz, discussion, sim)

1. **5 Cs first.** Especially customer (who / done / why), competition at all four levels, and whether the company can actually deliver.
2. **Segment** on needs, then describe with demographics, firmographics, RFM, etc. Test: identifiable, substantial, accessible, stable, differentiable, actionable.
3. **Target** on the three-way fit. Write down who you are **not** serving.
4. **Position** in one sentence: target + frame + difference + reason to believe.
5. **Only then 4 Ps.** Every mix choice should be defendable as “this is how we make that sentence true and get paid for it.”

If a 4P decision cannot be traced back to a target and a point of difference, it is not strategy. It is activity.
